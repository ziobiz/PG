API Reference · Online payments

    
Accept payments from your own checkout and confirm every result by webhook

    
This reference covers the full online payment path: authenticate, create a charge, let your customer pay, receive the result, and refund when needed. Every endpoint is JSON over HTTPS.

What this version covers

    
You build your own checkout and create each payment with the 
Charges API
. We process the payment, handle any redirect or QR step, and send you a webhook with the result. Refunds, events and reporting work the same way for every payment method.

    

Hosted payment pages are not part of this release.
 Every card payment therefore runs through your own checkout, which affects your PCI DSS scope — see 
Security &amp; compliance
.

  

  

    

      
Getting started

      
Authentication

      
Authenticate every request with HTTP Basic Auth. Use your 
accountId
 as the username and the key for the call as the password. Requests without credentials, or sent over plain HTTP, are rejected.

Acting on behalf of a sub-merchant

      
Use the sub-merchant's 
accountId
 as the username, your platform key as the password, and your platform ID in 
GW-Platform-Id
. Everything the request creates belongs to that sub-merchant's account.

      

Keep secret and platform keys out of front-end code, mobile app bundles and source control.
 If a key is exposed, rotate it immediately under 
Developers → API keys
. You choose how long the old key keeps working.

How charges work

  

    
A charge is one attempt to pay, created by your call to 
POST /v1/charges
. Every payment becomes a charge, and the 
origin
 field records where it came from — 
api
 in this release.

    

pending

→

succeeded

|

failed

|

expired

    

succeeded
, 
failed
 and 
expired
 are final. A failed charge can't be retried, so create a new one. Refunds don't change a charge's status; they increase 
amountRefunded
.

    

transactionTime
 is set once, the moment a charge first reaches a final status, and never changes after that — use it to know when the payment was actually settled. 
updatedAt
 is different: it also moves whenever you 
PATCH
 
metadata
, so it can't tell you when a charge became final.

    

Abandoned payments can stay 
pending
 for a long time
 — for example when a customer closes the QR screen or never opens their banking app. Set 
expiresAt
 when you create the charge, or call 
expire
 when your order times out. Never wait on a final status without a timeout of your own.

Failure codes

  

    

failure.code

Meaning

Suggested message to your customer

    

      

processing_error

The payment couldn't be processed.

"Something went wrong. Please try again."

Not enough funds.

"Please try another payment method."

      

authentication_failed

3-D Secure or OTP verification failed.

"We couldn't verify this payment. Please try again."

      

card_declined

The card issuer declined the payment.

"Your bank declined this card. Please contact them or use another card."

      

card_expired

The card has expired.

"This card has expired."

      

card_restricted

The card can't be used for this payment.

"Please use another card."

"Please check your card details."

 /

"This payment has expired. Please start again."
